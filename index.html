02 · BIOLOGY

How Aedes aegypti finds a human host

Host-seeking is a multisensory process. No single cue is enough by itself.

01

CO₂ · Long range

Gr2 + Gr3 form the functional core of the CO₂ receptor complex, while Gr1 modulates sensitivity. CO₂ can trigger upwind flight and gate responses to other host cues.

02

Skin odour · Mid range

Odorant receptors and ionotropic receptors detect human volatile compounds. IR8a is important for acidic cues such as lactic acid.

03

Thermal IR · Short–mid range

TRPA1-related sensory mechanisms, with opsin amplification, contribute to detection of thermal infrared from human targets.

04

Integration

CO₂, odour, visual information and thermal cues work together to guide the final approach.

CO₂→Skin odour→Thermal IR→Approach

Natural repellents

  • Local plant materials can reduce transport/manufacturing burden when locally sourced.
  • Generally more biodegradable.
  • Often shorter protection without formulation.
  • Frequent reapplication can change the overall environmental benefit.

Chemical repellents

  • Standardized active ingredients and generally longer protection.
  • More extensive safety testing for established products.
  • Some active chemicals have been detected in aquatic environments.
  • Impact depends on the specific chemical, use and disposal.

These are secondary data with different time windows and reporting methods, so they should not be treated as a complete small-area Jakarta risk map.

07 · PHYSICAL & HEALTH EDUCATION

Dengue affects school life too

PHE connects prevention with attendance, learning continuity, physical activity and health education.

Attendance

The PHE research reports evidence of missed school during illness and recovery. A Colombo study cited in the research found a mean of 4.6 school days missed from illness onset to discharge and 7.2 additional days from discharge to full recovery.

Learning continuity

Absence can mean missed lessons, practical work, quizzes and group activities. Catch-up work and clear communication can reduce disruption.

Physical activity

Recovery may reduce participation in PE and sport. A supportive, gradual return should follow family and health-professional advice.

Health education

Engaging, student-centred approaches can support knowledge and protective practices. Evaluation should measure behaviour as well as knowledge.

BeforeTeach dengue health literacy and prepare a continuity plan.

FINAL DIRECTION

A formulated, locally sourced citronella / lemongrass option

The project supports investigating formulated, locally sourced citronella or lemongrass repellents as a complementary prevention option, with formulation used to reduce rapid evaporation.

Important limit: repellents are not a replacement for wider dengue control, and this project is based on secondary research rather than live mosquito trials.
